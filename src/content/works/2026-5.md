My sculptural practice has moved away from discrete objects toward architectural fragments that emerge through growth, repetition and ongoing negotiation with material. Informed by biomimicry and the systems thinking of Jack Burnham, I understand structure as something that develops through the interplay of body, material and environment rather than through fixed design.

Inspired by termite mounds and ant colonies, where collective labour produces adaptive architectures, I approach clay as a responsive, living system. Gravity, moisture and drying shape the forms, producing sagging, shrinkage and cracks that I engage with rather than correct. I consider the fired sculptures fossilised moments, a snapshot of an ongoing process.
